**Tags** are short labels you put on an applicant, such as "Strong English". The **source** says where the person heard about the job. Both help you search and understand which ads work.

# Where the source comes from

The apply form asks **How did you hear about this job?** The question is optional. A link such as `/careers/your-job?src=linkedin` selects the answer for the person, so you can post one link for each place you advertise. Staff can change the source on the applicant's page.

# Open the panel

1. Open an applicant's page. See "Review applications and move them along".
2. Find **Source and tags**.

![Source and tags on an applicant's page.](help:tag-applicants-and-track-sources/01-source-and-tags.png)

# Change the source

1. Under **Heard about the job**, choose the answer: **Our careers page**, **Someone referred me**, **LinkedIn**, **Facebook**, **A job board**, **An agency or school**, **Somewhere else**, or **Not said**.
2. Add **More detail (optional)**, for example who referred them.
3. Click **Save source**. A message says **Source saved.**

# Add tags

1. Tick the tags you want under **Tags (up to 10)**.
2. To make a new tag, type it in **Make a new tag** and click **Add tag**.
3. Click **Save tags**. A message says **Tags saved.**

Tags are shared by the whole team. A tag name is unique, so "Strong English" and "strong english" are the same tag. Remove a tag by clearing its tick and saving.

# Tag many people at once

Use the bulk bar on the **Applicants** page. See "Act on many applicants at once".

# Find people by source or tag

On the **Applicants** page, open **More filters** and choose a **Source** or a **Tag**. See "Find applicants across all your jobs". The report **Where applicants come from** shows how many people from each source reached an interview and how many were hired. See "Read the recruiting reports".

# Common mistakes

- **Using a tag for something private.** Tags are visible to everyone who can see applicants. Never use them for health, family, religion or anything about the person that is not about the work.
- **Making many near-copies of the same tag.** Check the list first.
- **Writing client or patient information.** Never.

# Try it yourself

- Open a test applicant and set their source to **LinkedIn**.
- Add two tags and save.
- Find them again with the **Tag** filter.
