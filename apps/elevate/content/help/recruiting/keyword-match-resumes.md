The **keyword match** is a reading aid. You list the words you look for in resumes for a job, such as "Medical billing". When someone applies, ELEVATE counts which of those words appear in their resume and shows a percentage, the words it found and the words it did not.

It is only a hint to help you read faster. It never rejects anyone, and people always decide. It uses nothing about the person except the words in their resume. ELEVATE reads the resume on its own server and does not keep the resume text. Only the percentage and the found and missing words are kept.

# Step 1: List the keywords for a job

1. Open the job from **Recruiting**, then click **Edit job**.
2. Find **Resume keywords (optional)**.
3. In **Must have**, list the words that matter most. In **Nice to have**, list the extras. Separate them with commas or put each on its own line.
4. Click **Save job**.

Must-have words count for 70% of the score. Nice-to-have words count for 30%. Keep each list short and specific. You can list up to 30 words in a box, and each word can be up to 40 letters.

![The job form. Must have and Nice to have lists sit under Resume keywords.](help:keyword-match-resumes/01-job-keywords.png)

# Step 2: Read an applicant's match

Open an applicant's page. See "Review applications and move them along". The **Keyword match** box shows:

- the percentage and a word: **Strong** (75% or more), **Fair** (50% or more) or **Weak**.
- **Found in the resume** and **Not found**.
- the date it was scored.

If the box says **No score: the resume has no readable text**, the resume may be a scanned picture or a damaged file. Read it yourself. It is not a bad sign about the person.

![An applicant's keyword match: the percentage, the words found and the words missing.](help:keyword-match-resumes/02-keyword-match.png)

The match also shows as **Match 85%** on the **Applicants** page, and you can filter by it. See "Find applicants across all your jobs".

# Step 3: Read the resumes again after you change the words

If you change a job's keywords, older scores still use the old words, and the applicant's page says **Keywords changed since**. To update everyone:

1. Open the job's board from **Recruiting**.
2. Click **Re-score resumes**. The button says **Scoring...** while it works.

# Common mistakes

- **Treating the percentage as a decision.** A low match is not a reason to reject. Read the resume.
- **Listing too many words.** The score becomes hard to use. Choose the few that really matter.
- **Using words about the person.** The list is for skills and experience only, never age, gender, nationality or anything personal.
- **A Word (DOC) or scanned resume with no score.** Read it by hand.

# Try it yourself

- Add three must-have words to a test job and save.
- Open a test applicant and read the keyword match.
- Change a word, then click **Re-score resumes**.
