## The Jibble link (Phase 2.4)

**The Tracker App replaces Jibble screenshots (2026-11-23).** The Jibble link steps aside for anyone whose work computer is connected to the ELEVATE Tracker App (an active `time.tracker_devices` row), so the same person is not watched twice. One rule, `jibble/applies.ts` (`jibbleAppliesSql`, `trackerReplacesJibble`): the link applies when the team switch is on AND (no connected computer OR Jibble is running right now, meaning the last call sent to Jibble for the person was a start or a break end). It is used by `rulesFor` (so `performClock` queues nothing and the nightly "no screenshots" flag is not raised), `maySendNow`, the repair sweep, the nightly comparison and the working-mismatch check. A queued call is dropped (`skipped`, "not sent: the Tracker App is connected") if the computer was connected after it was queued, except a call that stops a Jibble that is already running, so connecting a computer mid-shift never leaves Jibble running. Revoking every computer of a person (or signing it out) brings the Jibble link back for them if their team still has it on. Jibble that a person starts by hand in their own Jibble app is outside ELEVATE: HR must tell them to stop using it. Tests: `tests/integration/jibble.test.ts` ("people who use the ELEVATE Tracker App are not also run in Jibble").

**Implementation status (October 27, 2026):** The tabbed page, shift summary, retry restrictions, viewer-timezone display and server-side history filtering are implemented. Additional coordination and mismatch workflows remain experimental and are disabled by default.

The in-progress operation guard provides a durable, per-person reservation and records an in-flight or uncertain phase. A guarded sender and mismatch checker are available only behind the experimental `JIBBLE_COORDINATION_ENABLED=true` switch. Do not enable it for production until the remaining coordination, recovery and rollout checks are complete. Reservations are not automatically stolen after a timeout. With the switch off, the existing repair behavior remains in effect and separate workers can overlap.

An in-progress mismatch lifecycle records mismatches separately and deduplicates notifications per incident and recipient, including a newly assigned time lead. Its local checker rechecks mapping, queued calls, monitoring eligibility and ELEVATE clock state. Under the experimental switch, the Inngest schedule targets five minutes and rotates through at most 150 candidates per run; job delays, capacity and the 15-minute backstop mean this is not a detection guarantee. A recent unmodified ELEVATE web clock-out can trigger a guarded Jibble stop when a verified older Jibble entry is still active; ambiguous and newer entries go to review. HR can mark a failed call reviewed only when current states are confirmed to match; the failure and audit entry remain. For eligible uncertain In/Out requests, HR can confirm the exact pre-recorded ID in Jibble without resending it; if it is absent, changed or still in flight, the guard remains blocked. This does not assume the provider makes repeated requests idempotent.

The experimental checker can create a pending, system-origin correction only for a recently observed Jibble Desktop clock-in when ELEVATE is out and the source entry can be re-read unchanged, its creation and event times are close, it has not been updated, no ambiguous ELEVATE send is found, and the proposed event passes attendance validation. Entries that do not meet these checks remain review issues. Source identity is unique, the employee is not impersonated, and HR approval rechecks the source entry and current Jibble state before writing attendance events. A Jibble-reported client type is operational evidence, not cryptographic proof of who used a device.

For a possible Jibble-only start, HR must investigate the entry and record review findings before closing the issue. Closure also requires matching current statuses and no pending time correction; the review and its note remain in the audit trail. This does not itself approve or create payable time.

On October 27, 2026, a read-only check of the designated test account observed a user-initiated Jibble Desktop clock-in with an entry ID and timezone-qualified timestamps. The tester reported that desktop tracking appeared active and a screenshot was recorded during that desktop-initiated session. Controlled API clock-ins confirmed that Jibble returned a client-supplied ID and, with Jibble Desktop open, the tester reported active tracking and a new screenshot. After an API clock-out, a state lookup showed out and the tester confirmed that desktop tracking stopped. These observations do not establish duplicate-request idempotency, how edited, offline or delayed entries are represented, or a complete ELEVATE-to-desktop end-to-end test.

- **Jibble is only for screenshots.** ELEVATE is the time clock and the only source of hours. Never store Jibble screenshots, GPS or activity. The integration reads Jibble's latest clock state and daily tracked-minute totals for the nightly comparison (`time.jibble_daily`, kept 35 days, never used for hours or exports).
- **Credentials:** a personal access token (`JIBBLE_ACCESS_TOKEN`, works on Jibble's free plan and acts as the person who made it) or a Client ID + Secret. Local tests use the git-ignored `apps/elevate/.env.jibble.local`; production uses the host's environment. No token = nothing is sent and the clock works as usual. A rejected token (401/403) alerts HR at most once in two hours. Never log tokens; failures store only a short code and the HTTP status (`describeFailure`).
- **Code:** `src/modules/jibble/`. `http-client.ts` is plain fetch (no server-only, so `pnpm jibble:probe` can use it): Workspace `/v1/People`, TimeTracking `POST /v1/TimeEntries` (type In, Out or StartBreak) and `/v1/TimeEntries/EndBreak`, TimeAttendance `/v2/TimesheetsSummary`. `client.ts` holds the configured client and `setJibbleClient()` for tests (use a fake in every test; never call the real API from tests). `mirror-rules.ts` is the pure part.
- **Mirror (outbox):** `enqueueMirror()` writes `time.jibble_link_log` rows (one per clock event) in the clock transaction when a client is configured, mirroring is enabled, the monitoring policy is published, and the person's team has `clock_rules.jibble_mirror` on (with the documented self-test exception). `processMirrorQueue()` orders each person's queued calls within one run; separate workers are not yet serialized and can overlap. Transient errors are retried with backoff, and a retry waits if Jibble's state cannot be confirmed. A 409 is marked sent only after a state check confirms the target. Unmatched people get a `skipped` row. Jibble being down never blocks the ELEVATE clock.
- **Breaks:** `JIBBLE_BREAK_MODE` = `native` (the default: Jibble's own breaks), `clock` (break start sends Out, break end sends In) or `off`. Native mode needs **breaks set up in Jibble** (Settings > Breaks, available through each person's schedule; make them Unpaid, since ELEVATE's breaks are deducted). Jibble lists breaks **per person** (`GET /v1/GetBreaks(personId,time)`); `pickBreak` prefers unpaid, exact length, then a flexible break, the shortest long enough, or the longest. If a native break start is refused for a non-retryable, non-auth reason, the integration attempts a clock-out instead; the call can still fail if that fallback is rejected. A clock-out on a break sends only Out in clock mode and EndBreak then Out in native mode. Corrections are never mirrored.
- **Send-now and late sending:** after the clock transaction commits, `kickMirror` schedules an immediate send through Next.js `after`; outside request scope it asks Inngest when configured, with scheduled sweeps as a backstop. Delays have multiple possible causes. `isSendingSlow` flags an aggregate of recent first-attempt sends over three minutes. A sent call or a Jibble clock-in does not verify that the desktop app captured screenshots.
- **Self-test** (`JIBBLE_SELF_TEST_EMAILS`, a deployment setting, comma-separated sign-in emails): a listed person is mirrored to Jibble **without** the published policy and the team switch, so the owner can test the link on their own account. Off when empty (the normal state). It is an environment setting on purpose, never a screen, so HR cannot use it to monitor anyone; the Jibble page shows an amber "Self-test is on" notice while it is set. Remove it when testing is done. Still needs `JIBBLE_ACCESS_TOKEN`, `ELEVATE_ENV=production`, a matched Jibble person and a configured client. The repair job covers listed people too; the nightly comparison does not.
- **Fallback mode** (`JIBBLE_MIRROR_ENABLED=false`): ELEVATE sends nothing, people clock into both apps, and the nightly comparison finds gaps.
- **People:** `time.jibble_people` (own table, not a column on employees) matched by work email nightly (`syncJibblePeople`) or by hand in the Jibble page; a hand-made match is never overwritten and one Jibble account is never given to two people. **Matching by hand** (Attendance > Jibble, `setJibblePerson`): HR types the email the person uses in Jibble, their Jibble member code (as in Jibble's exports), or Jibble's person id; the server looks it up in Jibble's people list (`match-rules.ts`, whole value, any case) and stores the id it found. No match, or more than one, is refused with a message; the browser never supplies an id the server did not look up (except a pasted id, as before). The member code is read through `$select=...,code`; if Jibble refuses that field (400) the client carries on without codes and email matching still works.
- **Nightly comparison** (`runJibbleComparison`, 3:10 AM Phoenix, after the 2:30 rebuild): for people on teams with the switch on, yesterday in the person's own zone; ELEVATE minutes = worked minutes (plus break minutes when breaks are not sent); more than `JIBBLE_MISMATCH_MINUTES` (default 15) apart sets the `jibble_mismatch` day flag (also on a day with no ELEVATE record). The nightly rebuild keeps the flag.
- **Jibble page:** `jibble.manage` is for HR and Super Admin. Connection controls sit above tabs for ELEVATE shift times, individual call history and people matching. The local page shows the viewer's attendance timezone (company default when absent) and offers server-side name/status filtering and 25-row pagination over retained calls. Nonqueued calls are purged after 90 days; queued calls are retained. Manual retry is limited to recent failed event calls with no other call at the same or a later creation time; it checks current Jibble state and reports the result. This restriction does not resolve the concurrent-worker or stale-backlog risks.
- `pnpm jibble:probe [email] [--clock] [--native-break]` checks the token and tries one TEST person; use it before turning a team on to see whether an API clock-in really starts the desktop app's screenshots.

## Hardening before the pilot (Phase 2.6, part 1)

- **Safety lock** (`sendsAllowed()` in `jibble/client.ts`): ELEVATE sends to Jibble only when `ELEVATE_ENV=production`. Anywhere else (laptops, staging, tests) it sends only for Jibble person ids in `JIBBLE_TEST_PERSON_IDS`; other calls are logged as skipped ("not sent: not the production environment"). The live Jibble organization is real people: **set `ELEVATE_ENV=production` only on the production deployment**, and tests that need sending set it themselves.
- **Repair job** (`runJibbleRepair`, every 30 minutes; the backstop also runs it every 15): for eligible people who are working or recently clocked out, compare ELEVATE's state with Jibble's latest time entry and send a repair if needed. It skips people with a waiting call, a click in the last 3 minutes, or a repair in the last 9 minutes, and does nothing while paused; at most 150 people a run. Repairs have `source = 'repair'` and no event. A retry checks Jibble's state, but the current implementation does not guarantee against concurrent or uncertain duplicate sends.
- **Break fallback:** a native break start that Jibble refuses (no break for the person, already used; not auth, not a retryable error) sends a clock-out instead (`fallback = true` on the row) so screenshots still stop; its break end then sends a clock-in.
- **Pause switch** (`time.jibble_settings`, `setJibblePaused`, HR): while paused, new calls wait; a request already in flight may still complete. In the existing path, resuming drops calls that waited over 10 minutes and runs repair. Under the experimental coordination switch, old calls remain queued for review and the non-restarting mismatch check runs instead; recovery is not yet complete.
- **Alerts:** auth failures alert HR at most every 2 hours (`jibble.auth`); 3 or more failed calls in 2 hours alert at most every 6 hours (`jibble.failing`); the Jibble page shows a banner when the oldest waiting call is 15 minutes old. A 429 waits at least its `Retry-After`. One token request at a time per server. **Unmatched people** (`runJibbleUnmatchedReport`, daily): HR gets one list of who clocked in with no Jibble account yesterday; the day gets the `no_screenshots` flag (not for the safety-lock skip).
- **The clock never depends on the rate limiter:** `clock` and `presence` fail open when Upstash is missing or unreachable (`allowWhenLimiterUnavailable`); every other policy still fails closed in production. The header says "Cannot reach ELEVATE: refresh, or sign in again" when the browser is online but requests fail.
- **Payroll completeness:** every Monday 8 AM Manila each lead is told how many of their people have unapproved hours from last week (HR for people with no lead); Wednesdays HR gets who is holding it up. The Hours export tab shows approved versus not approved days by team for the latest periods, and an export tells HR how many finished days it left out. "Absent" is labelled "Absent (review)": never an automatic deduction.

## Hours from Jibble (the Jibble clock; owner decision 2026-11-14)

- **Who:** HR sets a person's clock source on the Jibble page (`setClockSource`, `jibble.manage`; `time.clock_source` is append-only, the newest row decides, default ELEVATE). Needs a Jibble match, and the person must be clocked out. The person is notified. They then cannot use the ELEVATE clock (`performClock` refuses, the header says "You clock in Jibble"), are never mirrored to Jibble (`maySendNow`), are left out of the nightly comparison and quiet-session alerts, and get no `no_screenshots` or `jibble_mismatch` flags.
- **Import** (`jibble/import.ts`, job `jibble-import` every 30 minutes, also "Import now" per person for 1 to 14 days): reads the person's active time entries (`listTimeEntries`: type, time, project id, activity id; nothing else) for the last 3 days and makes ELEVATE events with `source = 'jibble'` and `jibble_entry_id`. In while out = clock-in; In while on a break = end of break; In while working with another client = `switch_client` (how Jibble records a change of project or activity); start break = break start; Out = clock-out (ending a break first). `import-rules.ts` holds the pure rules (`desiredEvents`, `planImport`).
- **Edits and deletions** are followed on the next import: the old event is **voided** (never edited) and a new one added; an entry deleted in Jibble voids its events. Uniqueness is (entry id, type, time, client), so an edit is a new row. An event voided earlier (by HR, or by an edit) is not added again while the entry still says the same. A day already approved shows "changed after approval".
- **Safety:** if Jibble returns no entries for someone who already has imported hours in the window, nothing is voided and HR is told once a day (a hiccup is more likely than every entry being deleted). A 401 or 403 alerts HR. Nothing runs while the link is paused, when Jibble is not configured, or (outside production) for people not in `JIBBLE_TEST_PERSON_IDS`.
- **Clients:** for a person with one client every entry belongs to it. With several, HR maps Jibble projects and activities to clients (`jibble_client_map`; an activity wins over a project). Ids with no client are listed for HR (`jibble_unmapped_ids`, ids only), the time has no client, and the day is flagged `needs_client` (blocks approval) until mapped; the next import re-attributes the day.
- **Corrections** still work: HR's correction writes `admin_correction` events or voids imported ones; the import leaves those alone. Corrections for Jibble people are better made in Jibble so the next import picks them up.
- **Not verified against live Jibble:** whether time entries carry `projectId` and `activityId` on our plan. The client asks for them and, if Jibble refuses the select (400), carries on without them (then only people with one client get a client). Try one person with `JIBBLE_TEST_PERSON_IDS` before moving everyone.
- **Moving many people at once** (`setJibbleClockForMany`, `jibble.manage`): the Jibble page lists matched people who have not used the ELEVATE clock for 14 days and are clocked out ("People who seem to use Jibble alone"; `jibbleAlone` in `clock-source-queries.ts`). HR ticks and confirms; each person goes through the same rule as a single change (`applyClockSource`: matched, clocked out, audited, notified) and anyone who cannot move is skipped with the reason. Afterwards the job event `jibble/import.requested` (`employeeIds`, `days: 14`) imports their last 14 days; without `INNGEST_EVENT_KEY` the 30-minute job reads 3 days.
- **A session Jibble left open** (owner request 2026-11-24): a person on the Jibble clock whose last Jibble entry is an In with no Out (the timer was never stopped; closing the Jibble app does not stop it) shows as clocked in in ELEVATE, cannot use the ELEVATE clock, and so could never be moved back (`applyClockSource` needs them clocked out). The Jibble page now shows "Clocked in since ..." per person, the refusal says what to do, and HR and Super Admin have **Close session and switch** (`closeSessionAndUseElevateClock`): HR enters the time the person really stopped and a reason; the session is closed with `admin_correction` events (a break end first when on a break, then the clock-out), an approved `clock_corrections` row and the audit action `clock_source.close_session`, the person is told, and in the same transaction they move to the ELEVATE clock. It uses the same rules as "change someone's time" (`checkProposal`: not in the future, after their last event, a session of 16 hours at most, a year back at most) and nobody does it to themselves. Someone with nothing open just switches. It does not touch Jibble: if their Jibble timer is still running, ask them to stop it there too.
- **The import job runs in small steps** (`jibble-import`: a list step, then 8 people per step, from the schedule or the event), so a long list never meets one function's time limit. `runJibbleImport` takes `employeeIds` for a subset.
